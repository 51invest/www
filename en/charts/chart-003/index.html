    Research review

    AI-only review · Doubao (AI) · 2026-11-22 · Verdict: passed

    This chart places U.S. unemployment and CPI annual inflation in two stacked panels to show how a labour-market indicator and a price indicator move over the same period. It does not draw a Phillips curve or claim a stable trade-off.

    • Data and sources：Unemployment is FRED UNRATE (originally BLS, seasonally adjusted); CPI is the BIS monthly annual rate. 32 months, 2024-01 to 2026-09; 2025-10 is excluded because BIS has no value. Unemployment ranges 3.7%–4.5%, CPI 2.31%–4.25%.
    • Method and definitions：Both series use a common monthly sample and drop missing months entirely, with no interpolation. 32 months are insufficient to estimate a stable Phillips-curve relationship, so the page presents them side by side and computes neither a slope nor an equilibrium unemployment rate.
    • Chart design and accessibility：Unemployment and inflation use different units, so the chart uses stacked panels with separate vertical axes; panels compare movement only, never levels. The caption and data table keep this note.
    • English wording：The English page names the “unemployment rate” and the “CPI annual rate” separately; unemployment is not conflated with jobless claims.

    Residual risks and scope limits

    • The 32-month sample cannot identify the slope or stability of a Phillips curve.
    • Unemployment and CPI differ in definition, adjustment and coverage; panels show direction, not level differences.
