What this route solves

Three objectives, three kinds of judgement. The goal is not to recall terms but to explain a case, carry out a calculation and state a limit in order.

  • Distinguish assets from instruments
  • Understand equity and bond rights
  • Compare ways to hold gold

Core route

Each step answers one sub-question. A concept introduced earlier is used again later; the order comes from the register, not from popularity.

01 Assets and Instruments Assets answer what stores value; instruments define the legal claims and cash flows used to hold it, so the same underlying can carry different rights and risks. MKT-001 · · 14 sections · 8 min 02 Cash and Money-Market Instruments Cash, deposits, Treasury bills, commercial paper, CDs, repos and money market funds all offer short-term liquidity, but their issuers, rights, exits and risks differ. MKT-002 · MKT-M01 · 15 sections · 13 min 03 Common and Preferred Shares Both are equity claims on the same company, but voting power, dividend priority, liquidation order and exit terms differ under the charter and offering documents. MKT-003 · MKT-M02 · 16 sections · 13 min 04 Bond Rights and Cash Flows A bond is a debt claim; holders receive coupons and principal under the offering documents, while coupon, price, yield and risk are distinct ideas. MKT-004 · MKT-M04 · 15 sections · 19 min 05 Comparing Ways to Hold Gold Physical gold, gold ETFs and gold futures all provide gold exposure, but their legal claims, cash flows, leverage and risks differ. Extension by module

These articles do not take a core-step slot; they fill in the station’s concepts, methods and historical background by module. Read the route first, then jump as needed.

E01 Repo, Money Funds and Liquidity Tiers Short-term cash instruments all look cash-like, but collateral, redemption, valuation and counterparties differ; liquidity tiers emerge under stress. MKT-032 · MKT-M01 · 19 sections · 9 min E02 Equity Financing, Governance and Shareholder Rights Equity financing trades ownership for capital; dilution, voting, preferences and governance terms determine what existing holders give up. MKT-036 · MKT-M02 · 19 sections · 16 min E03 Index Construction: Free Float and Benchmark Selection An index is not the market itself but a replicable set of eligibility, weighting, capping and rebalancing rules; benchmark choice changes attribution. MKT-035 · MKT-M03 · 19 sections · 8 min E04 Credit Spreads, Duration and Recovery Credit bonds yield more than risk-free rates to compensate default, uncertain recovery, liquidity and terms; duration amplifies rate and spread moves. MKT-038 · MKT-M04 · 19 sections · 9 min E05 Chemicals, Rubber and Materials Chemicals, rubber and materials are intermediate-goods markets linking energy, manufacturing and construction; prices reflect capacity cycles, feedstock costs and logistics radius. MKT-025 · MKT-M09 · 13 sections · 18 min E06 Chemical Value Chains: Feedstock, Spreads and Inventory Chemical value is set not by one price but by spreads across feedstock, processing, grade, region and inventory. MKT-029 · MKT-M09 · 17 sections · 9 min E07 Environmental, Freight and Specialty Markets Environmental and freight markets trade rights, capacity or services rather than standardised goods; standards, compliance and market depth determine whether prices can be interpreted. MKT-026 · MKT-M11 · 12 sections · 9 min E08 Environmental Capacity and Specialised Markets: Pricing Carbon, Water and Weather Environmental capacity is a scarce resource: emission rights, water rights and weather risk can be measured, allocated and traded. Specialised markets turn “who may use how much, who bears weather losses” into prices, but whether prices work depends on caps, monitoring and compliance. MKT-040 · MKT-M11 · 14 sections · 8 min E09 FX Regimes, NDFs and Cross-Border Hedging The exchange-rate regime determines which tools are available; NDFs cash-settle around delivery restrictions, but basis, regulation and counterparty risk remain. MKT-033 · MKT-M12 · 19 sections · 9 min E10 Futures Basis, Rolls and Delivery The gap between futures and spot, the cost of rolling and delivery rules jointly determine hedging and holding returns; a futures price move alone is not enough. MKT-031 · MKT-M13 · 19 sections · 8 min E11 Option Pricing and the Volatility Surface Option prices separate direction, time, volatility and rates; implied volatilities across strikes and expiries form a surface. MKT-034 · MKT-M14 · 21 sections · 13 min E12 Swap Pricing and Structured Tranching A swap exchanges two cash-flow streams; a structured product then slices underlying assets into different priority claims. Pricing centres on net present value, counterparty and terms. MKT-037 · MKT-M15 · 19 sections · 21 min E13 REITs and Infrastructure: Cash Flow, Leverage and Valuation REITs package rental cash flows from real estate into tradable shares; infrastructure funds package long-term cash flows from tolls, networks and power plants into similar structures. Both attract investors with stable cash flow and are constrained by rates, leverage and asset quality. MKT-041 · MKT-M16 · 15 sections · 8 min E14 Private Funds and Hedge Funds: Fees, Lock-ups and Liquidity Private funds and hedge funds sell not a stock but a strategy, a fee schedule and a liquidity arrangement. 2/20 fees, lock-ups, side pockets and redemption gates determine net returns and whether investors can exit in a crisis. MKT-042 · MKT-M17 · 21 sections · 9 min E15 Retirement Products and Accounts Retirement products have three layers: account rules, product structure and decumulation; a change in any layer changes final income and risk. MKT-027 · MKT-M19 · 12 sections · 11 min E16 Retirement Products and Accounts: Annuities, Target-Date Funds and Tax Advantages Retirement products solve “will there be cash flow after retirement”; accounts solve “which tax container holds the money”. Annuities turn savings into lifetime income, target-date funds glide allocation with age, and tax-advantaged accounts rearrange contribution and withdrawal timing. MKT-043 · MKT-M19 · 14 sections · 8 min E17 Stablecoins, Custody and On-Chain Settlement Risk A stablecoin is an issuer liability, not a bank deposit; reserves, redemption rights, custody and on-chain finality determine whether it holds its peg under stress. MKT-039 · MKT-M20 · 19 sections · 9 min E18 Collectibles and Other Alternatives Alternative collectibles are priced through authenticity, rights, custody and liquidity; without enforceable rights and verifiable provenance, a price is only a story. MKT-028 · MKT-M21 · 12 sections · 9 min E19 Collectibles: Valuation, Authenticity and Liquidity Collectible prices reflect scarcity, provenance and demand, but authenticity, transaction costs and exit liquidity determine what a buyer actually gets. MKT-030 · MKT-M21 · 17 sections · 9 min E20 Indices and Investment Benchmarks An index is a rules-based measure of market performance, not a directly held product; a benchmark is the reference used to judge a portfolio, and both must be checked for method and basis. MKT-005 · MKT-M03 · 15 sections · 12 min E21 Fund Structures Funds pool investor money under a contract; legal form, share rights, valuation and exit mechanics matter more than the label. MKT-006 · MKT-M05 · 16 sections · 23 min E22 ETF Creation, Redemption and Premiums ETFs trade at market prices while authorized participants create and redeem shares in creation units, usually priced at NAV; arbitrage usually narrows premiums but does not guarantee it. MKT-007 · MKT-M05 · 16 sections · 9 min E23 Silver's Industrial and Investment Roles Silver is consumed by electronics, photovoltaics and brazing, and also held as an investment; industrial and investment demand together affect its price and risks. MKT-010 · MKT-M06 · 14 sections · 8 min E24 Copper and Industrial Metals Copper underlies construction, power grids, transport and electronics; physical metal, futures and mining shares carry different rights and risks. MKT-011 · MKT-M07 · 14 sections · 11 min E25 The Steel Supply Chain From iron ore, coking coal and scrap to crude steel, hot-rolled coil and rebar, cash flows, costs and risks pass through each processing stage. MKT-012 · MKT-M07 · 14 sections · 7 min E26 Crude Oil and Refined Products Crude oil is refinery feedstock and refined products are its outputs; crude futures, product futures and refining margins carry different rights and cash flows. MKT-013 · MKT-M08 · 15 sections · 20 min E27 Natural Gas and Electricity Markets Natural gas is a storable, transportable commodity, while electricity is a secondary energy source that must be balanced in real time; their contracts, costs and risks differ. MKT-014 · MKT-M08 · 15 sections · 15 min E28 Grains, Oilseeds and Agricultural Supply Grains and oilseeds are priced by variety, grade and delivery period; weather, stocks, crush margins and basis shape the cash flows and risks of the agricultural chain. MKT-015 · MKT-M10 · 16 sections · 14 min E29 Soft Commodities and Livestock Coffee, cocoa, sugar and cotton respond to origin weather and grading, while cattle and hogs respond to biological cycles and feed costs; their contracts and delivery differ. MKT-016 · MKT-M10 · 14 sections · 9 min E30 FX Quotes and Trading Instruments FX is quoted in currency pairs; spot, forward, swap, futures and option instruments carry different rights, cash flows, margin and counterparty risks. MKT-017 · MKT-M12 · 15 sections · 14 min E31 Futures Contracts and Margin Futures are exchange-standardized contracts; both sides post margin and are marked to market daily, so leverage, margin calls and delivery rules shape losses and exits. MKT-018 · MKT-M13 · 16 sections · 13 min E32 Options: Rights, Obligations and Payoffs An option buyer pays a premium for a right, while the writer receives the premium and owes performance; strike, premium and expiry price determine the payoff. MKT-019 · MKT-M14 · 15 sections · 19 min E33 Swaps and Structured Products Swaps exchange cash flows rather than principal, while structured products combine debt with derivative terms; both depend on counterparties, collateral and offering documents. MKT-020 · MKT-M15 · 17 sections · 16 min E34 Real Estate and REITs Real estate can be owned directly or through REITs; a REIT's tax status depends on distribution, shareholder and asset tests, and listed and non-traded shares differ sharply in liquidity. MKT-021 · MKT-M16 · 16 sections · 12 min E35 Private Equity, Private Credit and Hedge Funds Hedge funds typically admit only accredited investors, and private credit funds are mostly closed-end and not open to retail investors; like private equity they are limited in liquidity, but their assets, leverage, cash flows and exit routes differ. MKT-022 · MKT-M17 · 17 sections · 14 min E36 Bank Wealth-Management and Trust Products Bank wealth-management and trust products are non-principal-guaranteed asset-management products with different issuers, legal relationships, investor thresholds, exit rules and disclosure requirements. MKT-008 · MKT-M18 · 16 sections · 11 min E37 Insurance and Retirement Products Insurance and retirement products both manage long-term risk, but insurance transfers specific event risk while retirement accounts provide tax and savings arrangements; they are not substitutes. MKT-023 · MKT-M18 · 15 sections · 9 min E38 Digital Assets and Digital Finance Digital assets are not one asset class; tokens, stablecoins, DeFi positions and central bank digital currencies differ in legal rights, custody and risk. MKT-024 · MKT-M20 · 17 sections · 14 min E39 The Carbon-Market Compliance Cycle: Allowances, MRV and Price Caps A carbon market is not simply emissions trading; it is a compliance cycle of allowance allocation, emissions monitoring, verification, surrender and penalties. Price caps, allowance reserves and stability mechanisms decide whether the carbon price fails under stress. MKT-044 · MKT-M11 · 13 sections · 8 min E40 Withdrawal Order and Annuitisation: The Retirement Withdrawal Ledger Retirement withdrawals are not just 'which account to spend first': pre-tax, after-tax, Social Security and annuities differ in tax, longevity and liquidity.
