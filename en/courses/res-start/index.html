What this route solves

Three objectives, three kinds of judgement. The goal is not to recall terms but to explain a case, carry out a calculation and state a limit in order.

  • Align units and definitions
  • Distinguish stocks and flows
  • Understand covariance and correlation

Core route

Each step answers one sub-question. A concept introduced earlier is used again later; the order comes from the register, not from popularity.

01 Data Units and Statistical Definitions Similar labels can hide different denominators, units, frequencies and seasonal adjustments; check definitions before comparing, and never fill missing values with zero. RES-001 · RES-M04 · 13 sections · 12 min 02 Stocks and Flows A stock is a balance at a point in time and a flow is a change over a period; treating a deficit as debt or income as wealth confuses accounting identities with causal claims. RES-002 · RES-M04 · 13 sections · 8 min 03 Frequency and Time Alignment Series at different frequencies must first share a reference period and an information set; aggregation is usually safer than disaggregation, and release times and time zones can change the date. RES-003 · RES-M04 · 13 sections · 8 min 04 Probability Distributions, Expectation and Variance A probability distribution assigns a probability to each possible outcome; expectation is the long-run average, while variance and standard deviation measure dispersion. They describe an assumed distribution, not the next outcome. RES-009 · RES-M02 · 14 sections · 9 min 05 Covariance and Correlation Covariance and correlation describe linear co-movement. They do not identify causation and cannot replace checks on samples, timing and outliers. RES-010 · RES-M02 · 13 sections · 8 min

Extension by module

These articles do not take a core-step slot; they fill in the station’s concepts, methods and historical background by module. Read the route first, then jump as needed.

E01 Mathematical Foundations Mathematics for research is not a pile of formulas; it turns a question into functions, constraints and computable steps, and tells you when approximations fail. RES-025 · RES-M01 · 13 sections · 8 min E02 Matrices, Calculus and Numerical Stability Matrix operations and calculus underpin quantitative analysis, but conditioning, scaling and floating-point error make the same formula produce different answers. RES-028 · RES-M01 · 23 sections · 8 min E03 Regression and Econometrics: Coefficients, Standard Errors and Causality Regression gives conditional association, not automatic causation. Coefficient size, standard errors, confidence intervals and model specification jointly determine the strength of a conclusion; shrinking standard errors or piling on controls can manufacture false certainty. RES-031 · RES-M06 · 22 sections · 10 min E04 Forecasting and Machine Learning The core of forecasting and machine learning is not model complexity but time boundaries, baselines, validation and drift monitoring. RES-026 · RES-M09 · 13 sections · 9 min E05 Forecasting and Machine Learning: Out-of-Sample, Overfitting and Baselines A forecasting model is judged out of sample, not on the training set. Baselines, rolling windows, overfitting and multiple comparisons determine whether it is useful; if a complex model loses to a simple baseline, complexity is a cost rather than an advantage. RES-032 · RES-M09 · 17 sections · 7 min E06 Event Studies: Windows, Abnormal Returns and Inference An event study subtracts model-expected return from realised return in an event window to obtain abnormal return; window choice and inference determine credibility. RES-030 · RES-M10 · 22 sections · 9 min E07 Data Versioning, Pipelines and Reproducible Experiments Reproduction is not rerunning a notebook; it means fixing data, code, environment and random seeds so every step is auditable. RES-029 · RES-M12 · 22 sections · 8 min E08 Alternative Data and Complex Systems Alternative data and complex systems expand what can be observed while magnifying representativeness, licensing and identification problems; the method's value lies in stating the limits. RES-027 · RES-M13 · 13 sections · 9 min E09 Alternative Data and Complex Systems: Signal, Noise and Networks Alternative data is not “more data” but data from a different generating process: satellite images, card transactions, job postings, shipping and social text. Complex systems remind us that variables have feedback, thresholds and network contagion, so linear extrapolation often fails. RES-033 · RES-M13 · 16 sections · 8 min E10 Research Reading and Writing: Question, Evidence and Reproduction Good research is not “opinion plus data” but a question that can be answered, evidence that can be checked and a conclusion with boundaries. When reading, start with the question and identification, then the conclusion; when writing, make data, method, robustness and limits reproducible. RES-034 · RES-M14 · 16 sections · 8 min E11 Sampling and Confidence Intervals A confidence interval combines a sample mean with sampling error to give a range for the population mean; 95% means about 95% of intervals from repeated samples would cover the mean, not that this interval has a 95% probability of containing it. RES-011 · RES-M03 · 15 sections · 11 min E12 Hypothesis Testing and Practical Significance Hypothesis testing treats “no difference” as the null and asks whether the sample supports rejecting it; a small p-value means the data are inconsistent with the null, not that the effect is large, important or real. RES-012 · RES-M03 · 16 sections · 9 min E13 Multiple Testing and Data Snooping Among 60 simulated rules with no predictive power, 4 look significant at the 5% level; under a Bonferroni threshold of 0.000833 none survive, while the chance of at least one false positive is 95.4%. RES-023 · RES-M03 · 13 sections · 9 min E14 Seasonal Adjustment and Revisions Seasonal adjustment strips out calendar-driven swings, and the adjusted numbers are themselves revised; a monthly change smaller than the typical revision is not yet evidence of a trend. RES-004 · RES-M04 · 13 sections · 8 min E15 Missing Data, Outliers and Sample Selection The same average growth rate changes with who is sampled, who responds, how missing values are filled and how outliers are treated; reporting the rule and a sensitivity range is more honest than reporting one precise number. RES-007 · RES-M04 · 13 sections · 8 min E16 Axes, Dual Axes and Misleading Charts Axes map values to positions, so changing an axis range or adding a second vertical axis can manufacture crossings and co-movement without touching the data. RES-005 · RES-M05 · 14 sections · 13 min E17 Indexing, Logarithms and Growth Rates An index turns a level into a ratio to a base period, and a logarithm turns that ratio into additive changes; growth rates, compound annual growth and doubling times are comparable only when definitions, base periods and price bases match. RES-006 · RES-M05 · 14 sections · 9 min E18 Introduction to Linear Regression Linear regression summarizes the average relationship between two variables with a line; slope, intercept and R-squared describe the sample, while significance and causal interpretation depend on design, assumptions and the limits of the evidence. RES-013 · RES-M06 · 14 sections · 9 min E19 Control Variables and Confounding Control variables are used to block confounding paths, not to include every available variable; a simple association can shrink after adjustment, but adjustment itself can introduce new bias. RES-014 · RES-M07 · 15 sections · 13 min E20 Causality and Identification Causal identification is not a significant regression; it maps observed data to a counterfactual through design or assumptions. Random experiments provide a benchmark, while observational methods each carry their own conditions. RES-015 · RES-M07 · 21 sections · 9 min E21 Trends, Nonstationarity and Spurious Regression Two nonstationary series with their own trends can produce a high R-squared and a significant slope even when they are independent; level-regression significance must be read alongside trends, autocorrelation and differenced results. RES-016 · RES-M08 · 14 sections · 12 min E22 Leads, Lags and Forecasting Cross-correlation and lagged regression can describe which series moves first, but transformations, windows and multiple tests all manufacture “leads”; forecasting skill must be judged by out-of-sample error. RES-017 · RES-M08 · 14 sections · 9 min E23 Rolling Analysis and Structural Change With 0.54 estimated in the first half and 0.20 in the second, a full-sample slope of 0.34 hides the break; rolling paths, break tests and out-of-sample forecasts must be reported together, with the window and break date explained - and a test that fails to reject may simply lack power. RES-018 · RES-M08 · 14 sections · 9 min E24 Event-Study Basics After removing normal returns with a market model, 40 hypothetical firms show a three-day average cumulative abnormal return of 1.9% with a t value of 4.8; a control window with no injected effect still reads 0.1% in this sample, which is the sampling noise such estimates carry before clustering and model choice are considered. RES-019 · RES-M10 · 15 sections · 14 min E25 Backtesting and Look-Ahead Bias A signal that includes the current month’s return earns 20.8% a year, while the version using only data through last month earns 11.4%; look-ahead inflates a constructed result, not skill, and the best of 1000 random strategies does not survive out of sample. RES-020 · RES-M11 · 14 sections · 11 min E26 Survivorship Bias and Trading Costs In a simulated 500-fund, 120-month universe, keeping only surviving funds lifts annualized return from 5.7% to 6.6%; a high-turnover strategy earns 9.4% gross but 7.96% after 30 bp one-way costs, only 0.22 percentage points above the low-turnover version. RES-021 · RES-M11 · 14 sections · 9 min E27 Out-of-Sample Testing and Overfitting Searching 500 parameter sets over 180 months gives a best in-sample Sharpe of 0.831 that falls to 0.072 out of sample; reusing the out-of-sample window for selection raises the reported value to 1.131, while the same combination falls to 0.489 over the final 60 months. RES-022 · RES-M11 · 15 sections · 10 min E28 A Reproducible Research Workflow Reproducibility is not “the code ran once”; it means another person can take the data version, code, environment and documentation and recompute the same table with one command. It guarantees recomputation, not correctness. RES-008 · RES-M12 · 14 sections · 11 min E29 Evidence Hierarchies and Research Reports Sampling 12 simulated studies by evidence tier gives realised average effects of 2.4% in tier A, 4.5% in B and 8.2% in C, 4.7% overall and 3.6% excluding the practitioner notes; a report should give tiers, a range and a certainty rating, not one headline number. RES-024 · RES-M14 · 14 sections · 12 min E30 Triangulation across Sources: Satellite, Text and Administrative Data Every single source has blind spots: satellites miss underground activity, text has selection bias and administrative data reflect reporting incentives. Triangulation is not 'using more sources' but cross-checking independent error structures to bound the true value. RES-035 · RES-M13 · 15 sections · 9 min
