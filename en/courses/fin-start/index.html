What this route solves

Three objectives, three kinds of judgement. The goal is not to recall terms but to explain a case, carry out a calculation and state a limit in order.

  • Understand money and credit
  • Apply time value and discounting
  • Read assets, liabilities and equity

Core route

Each step answers one sub-question. A concept introduced earlier is used again later; the order comes from the register, not from popularity.

01 Functions of Money Money is what is generally accepted in exchange and settlement. Its roles as a medium of exchange, unit of account and store of value lower transaction costs, but acceptance and stable value are conditions rather than guarantees. FIN-001 · FIN-M02 · 14 sections · 11 min 02 Credit Relationships A credit relationship moves purchasing power across time under contract: the lender gives up funds now, and the borrower promises to repay principal and interest later. Enforceability, information and collateral decide whether the relationship works. FIN-002 · FIN-M02 · 13 sections · 8 min 03 The Time Value of Money Cash flows at different dates cannot be added directly; compounding moves value forward and discounting brings it back to today. FIN-009 · FIN-M08 · 13 sections · 11 min 04 Simple and Compound Interest Simple interest is charged only on principal, while compound interest adds interest to principal and earns interest on it; the same rate and horizon can produce different future values and effective annual rates depending on the method and compounding frequency. FIN-010 · FIN-M08 · 14 sections · 9 min 05 Present Value and Discounted Cash Flows Present value converts future cash flows to today at a discount rate, and discounted cash flow sums those present values; the rate, timing and cash-flow assumptions jointly determine the result. FIN-011 · FIN-M08 · 14 sections · 10 min 06 The Accounting Equation Assets equal liabilities plus equity. The identity explains how resources are financed, but it does not mean assets are worth their market value or that a company is safe. FIN-017 · FIN-M10 · 11 sections · 10 min

Extension by module

These articles do not take a core-step slot; they fill in the station’s concepts, methods and historical background by module. Read the route first, then jump as needed.

E01 Introduction to Finance Finance is not about money making money; it rearranges savings, risk, maturity and information between surplus and deficit units. FIN-025 · FIN-M01 · 12 sections · 8 min E02 Financial Intermediation and Direct Financing: How Savings Become Investment Finance is not money breeding money. It rearranges scattered savings, risks and maturities and sends them to projects that need funding. Direct financing puts users of funds in front of investors; indirect financing has banks and other intermediaries collect deposits first and lend afterwards. The two routes allocate cost, information and risk differently. FIN-033 · FIN-M01 · 11 sections · 8 min E03 Financial Institutions Financial institutions differ not by sign but by balance sheet, maturity transformation, revenue source and regulatory perimeter. FIN-026 · FIN-M06 · 13 sections · 10 min E04 Financial Institution Balance Sheets and Maturity/Credit Transformation Financial institutions turn short-term liabilities into long-term assets, bearing maturity, credit and liquidity risk; capital and liquidity buffers determine how much shock they can absorb. FIN-032 · FIN-M06 · 17 sections · 9 min E05 Rate Conventions and Credit Spreads: Different Labels for the Same Price of Money The same 5% can be a nominal rate, a real rate, a coupon, a yield to maturity or an annual percentage rate. The difference is not a decimal point. Separate inflation, compounding frequency, credit and liquidity premia before comparing the cost of two funding options. FIN-034 · FIN-M07 · 12 sections · 8 min E06 Insurance and Pension Systems Insurance and pensions are both intertemporal risk arrangements: one pools uncertain individual losses into a priceable pool, the other moves income from working life to retirement. FIN-027 · FIN-M14 · 12 sections · 8 min E07 Insurance Pricing and Pension Liabilities: The Ledger of Risk Sharing Insurance does not abolish risk; it pools many small-probability, large-loss exposures into a predictable pool. Premium must cover expected claims, expenses and capital, while reserves must cover future benefits. Pensions separate current contributions from future promises; a funding gap is eventually closed by contributions, investment returns or public money. FIN-035 · FIN-M14 · 11 sections · 7 min E08 The International Financial System The international financial system is a cross-border arrangement of payments, credit and reserves; its efficiency comes from networks, and so does its fragility. FIN-028 · FIN-M15 · 12 sections · 9 min E09 Cross-Border Payments and Reserve Currencies: The Plumbing of International Finance A cross-border payment is not money “traveling” abroad; it is a chain of bookings across banks, clearing systems, correspondents and FX markets. A reserve currency is special because it serves as payment unit, funding currency and store of value at once. Reserve cover puts exchange-rate stability and import-paying capacity on one table. FIN-036 · FIN-M15 · 12 sections · 8 min E10 Foundations of Digital Finance Digital finance changes the technology layer of payments and settlement, but the liability, credit risk and legal rights of money still have to be identified item by item. FIN-029 · FIN-M16 · 12 sections · 9 min E11 Tokenized Deposits and Stablecoins: The Ledger of Payment Infrastructure Stablecoins and tokenized deposits both move “deposits” onto programmable ledgers, but the obligor, reserve assets and redemption rights differ. Safety cannot be read from the on-chain price alone; it depends on reserve cover, redemption order, settlement finality and who bears a run. FIN-037 · FIN-M16 · 13 sections · 8 min E12 Financial Stability Financial stability is the system's ability to keep payments, saving, borrowing and investment working through a shock without cascading failures, not the absence of volatility. FIN-030 · FIN-M17 · 12 sections · 8 min E13 Bank Runs, Leverage and Contagion: A Financial-Stability Stress Test Financial stability is not the absence of volatility; it is the system’s ability to keep payments and funding going under stress. A run is a liquidity problem, leverage amplifies losses, and contagion turns one failure into a network event. A stress test separates the three layers and puts them back on one balance sheet. FIN-038 · FIN-M17 · 20 sections · 10 min E14 Regulation, Law and Ethics Financial regulation protects enforceable rights and market integrity; ethics addresses judgment where the rulebook runs out. FIN-031 · FIN-M18 · 12 sections · 9 min E15 Disclosure, Suitability and Conflicts of Interest: Three Regulatory Ledgers Financial regulation is not just more forms. Disclosure addresses information asymmetry, suitability addresses the fit between product and client, and conflicts rules address who decides for whom. The three ledgers cover different gaps and cannot substitute for one another. Put client assets, market integrity and firm incentives on one table to judge whether a rule actually protects investors. FIN-039 · FIN-M18 · 13 sections · 8 min E16 Bank Balance Sheets A bank balance sheet lists reserves, loans and securities as assets and deposits and borrowings as liabilities, with equity as the difference; it shows that banks operate mainly with other people's money, and it explains why deposits are not the bank's own funds. FIN-003 · FIN-M03 · 12 sections · 10 min E17 Deposits, Lending and Credit Creation When a bank makes a loan it credits an equal deposit to the borrower, so money is created; credit creation is limited by reserves, capital, liquidity and loan demand, and the money multiplier is an upper bound rather than a forecast. FIN-004 · FIN-M03 · 13 sections · 10 min E18 Central Bank Balance Sheets A central bank balance sheet lists securities, lending and foreign reserves as assets and currency in circulation and bank reserves as liabilities; it records how the monetary base is created and shows why a central bank does not need deposits before expanding. FIN-005 · FIN-M04 · 13 sections · 10 min E19 Reserves and Liquidity Reserves are balances that banks hold at the central bank, while liquidity is a bank's ability to pay on time without unacceptable losses; reserves are only one layer of liquidity, and reserve requirements are no substitute for liquidity regulation. FIN-006 · FIN-M04 · 15 sections · 9 min E20 Payment, Clearing and Settlement Payment starts a transfer of value, clearing reconciles instructions and calculates what participants owe, and settlement completes the transfer with central bank money or eligible assets; netting saves liquidity but concentrates credit and liquidity risk in the clearing arrangement. FIN-007 · FIN-M05 · 13 sections · 11 min E21 Custody and Asset Rights Custody hands safekeeping, settlement and record-keeping to a specialist institution, but it does not by itself change beneficial ownership; segregation and the ability to exercise rights depend on the account structure, the agreement and applicable law. FIN-008 · FIN-M05 · 13 sections · 9 min E22 Interest-Rate Types and Conventions A quoted rate means little without its compounding, annualization and risk conventions; compare rates only on a consistent basis. FIN-012 · FIN-M07 · 13 sections · 13 min E23 Risk Premium A risk premium is the expected return above the risk-free rate; it compensates non-diversifiable risk, not every kind of volatility. FIN-013 · FIN-M09 · 13 sections · 10 min E24 The Law of One Price and No-Arbitrage Identical claims to future cash flows should have the same price; no-arbitrage turns that constraint into a pricing benchmark, though frictions let deviations persist. FIN-014 · FIN-M09 · 14 sections · 8 min E25 Introduction to Asset Pricing Models Asset pricing models map systematic risk to a required return, which is then used to discount cash flows; CAPM is the simplest one-factor benchmark. FIN-015 · FIN-M09 · 13 sections · 9 min E26 Accrual Accounting Accrual accounting recognizes revenue when earned and expenses when incurred, not when cash changes hands; it improves comparability across periods but relies on estimates, so profit and cash flow can diverge. FIN-020 · FIN-M10 · 12 sections · 9 min E27 The Income Statement The income statement summarizes revenue, expenses and profit over a period on an accrual basis; net income is not cash flow, and readers should separate gross profit, operating income and one-off items. FIN-018 · FIN-M11 · 12 sections · 10 min E28 The Cash-Flow Statement The cash-flow statement reports cash receipts and payments over a period in operating, investing and financing activities, and reconciles net income to operating cash flow under the indirect method; it complements the income statement but does not by itself establish a company's value. FIN-019 · FIN-M11 · 14 sections · 9 min E29 Cost of Capital The cost of capital is the return investors require to supply funds and the hurdle rate for projects; the weighted average cost of capital combines after-tax debt and equity costs at market-value weights and applies only to comparable-risk projects. FIN-016 · FIN-M12 · 13 sections · 11 min E30 Sources of Corporate Finance Companies fund assets with internal retained earnings or external debt and equity; the sources differ in claims, cost, control and maturity, and the choice should fit the target capital structure. FIN-021 · FIN-M12 · 12 sections · 9 min E31 Capital Structure Capital structure is the relative share of debt and equity financing; in perfect markets leverage does not change firm value, while tax shields and financial distress costs jointly set a target debt level. FIN-022 · FIN-M12 · 12 sections · 9 min E32 Dividends and Buybacks Dividends pay profit to shareholders in proportion to their holdings, while buybacks use cash to repurchase shares and cut the share count; both return cash but differ in per-share effects, execution rules and disclosure. FIN-023 · FIN-M13 · 12 sections · 10 min E33 Corporate Governance and Conflicts of Interest Corporate governance allocates rights and accountability among shareholders, the board, management and stakeholders; independent directors, committees, related-party disclosure and voting constrain conflicts of interest but do not eliminate agency costs. FIN-024 · FIN-M13 · 12 sections · 9 min E34 When Financial Functions Fail: Intermediation Cost Above Coordination Benefit The financial system exists to lower search, assessment, monitoring and settlement costs between savers and investors. When fragmentation, information asymmetry or regulatory friction push intermediation cost above coordination benefit, financial functions fail: cash sits idle while projects cannot borrow. This article uses a loan intermediation-cost account to separate the failure points of six functions. FIN-040 · FIN-M01 · 15 sections · 8 min E35 Constraints on Credit Creation: Capital, Liquidity and Loan Demand Banks do not simply 'take deposits and lend'; they create credit under three constraints: capital, liquidity and loan demand. This article uses a T-account and a capital-ratio example to explain why the same deposit lets one bank lend 90 yuan and another only 60. FIN-041 · FIN-M03 · 14 sections · 8 min E36 Ample Reserves and the Floor System: How the Corridor Works Under an ample-reserves framework, the central bank no longer fine-tunes rates through scarce reserves and open-market operations; it sets a floor with the reserve rate and ON RRP rate, letting market rates run inside a corridor. This article uses a reserve supply-demand table to explain why the floor system needs reverse repos and when it fails. FIN-042 · FIN-M04 · 21 sections · 11 min E37 Three Messages in the Yield Curve: Expectations, Risk and Liquidity An inverted yield curve is often read as a recession signal, but the curve carries at least three messages: future rate expectations, term risk premium and liquidity premium. Separating them tells whether inversion reflects tight policy or risk repricing. FIN-043 · FIN-M07 · 12 sections · 9 min E38 Accounting Estimates and Accruals: Where Earnings Quality Shows The same cash flow can produce very different net income under different depreciation lives, bad-debt provisions and warranty accruals. Accruals are not fraud, but they give management judgement room; to read earnings quality, trace where accruals go and whether they reverse. FIN-044 · FIN-M10 · 11 sections · 9 min E39 Three-Statement Articulation: Balance Sheet, Income Statement and Cash Flow Net income from the income statement enters retained earnings on the balance sheet, and the cash change from the cash-flow statement enters cash and equivalents. A few accounts link the three statements; when articulation fails, classification, consolidation scope or estimates are usually the cause. FIN-045 · FIN-M11 · 11 sections · 9 min E40 Buybacks versus Dividends: Same Cash Return, Different Signals Dividends are a stable cash commitment; buybacks are flexible capital allocation. Both reduce cash, but buybacks also change share count and per-share metrics. Do not read buybacks only through EPS accretion; check price, funding source and whether they offset equity-compensation dilution. FIN-046 · FIN-M13 · 12 sections · 9 min E41 Longevity Risk and Annuities: Converting Savings into Lifetime Income The point of an annuity is not high return but converting uncertain lifespan into certain income. It pools mortality risk across survivors but shifts inflation, fee and counterparty risk to the policyholder. FIN-047 · FIN-M14 · 12 sections · 9 min E42 Offshore Dollar Funding: Swaps, FX Swaps and Rollover Risk Offshore institutions need dollars but lack U.S. deposit insurance and the discount window; they obtain dollars through FX swaps, offshore deposits and central-bank swap lines. When dollars tighten, swap basis widens and rollover becomes difficult, transmitting stress back onshore. FIN-048 · FIN-M15 · 12 sections · 9 min E43 Stablecoin Reserves, Pegs and Runs: Is It a Bank? A stablecoin promises one coin for one dollar and holds reserves to defend the peg. It compresses deposit-reserve-redemption into an on-chain liability but lacks deposit insurance and a lender of last resort; reserve quality, maturity mismatch and redemption speed decide whether it runs. FIN-049 · FIN-M16 · 12 sections · 9 min E44 Wholesale Funding Runs: LCR, NSFR and Fire Sales Wholesale funding is not deposits, but it is more sensitive: repos, interbank certificates, commercial paper and bonds can stop rolling instantly under stress. LCR covers 30-day survival and NSFR the one-year structure; a bank can pass both and still run on collateral discounts and concentration. FIN-050 · FIN-M17 · 13 sections · 9 min E45 Client Asset Custody: Segregation, Rehypothecation and Institution Failure Client assets are not the institution's own assets; custody is about segregation and traceability. If client assets are commingled, rehypothecated or lent to counterparties, they become general claims in a failure. The longer the custody chain, the harder the recovery.
