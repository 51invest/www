What this route solves

Three objectives, three kinds of judgement. The goal is not to recall terms but to explain a case, carry out a calculation and state a limit in order.

  • Write goals and constraints
  • Understand return, risk and cost
  • Use correlation to understand diversification limits

Core route

Each step answers one sub-question. A concept introduced earlier is used again later; the order comes from the register, not from popularity.

01 Writing Investment Goals and Constraints Define the goal, amount, horizon, liquidity and risk capacity before discussing asset allocation; a goal statement is not a return promise. INV-001 · INV-M01 · 15 sections · 13 min 02 Return, Risk and Cost Trade-offs Compare net nominal return, risk and costs on one basis, using scenario ranges instead of a single expected value; all example numbers are hypothetical. INV-002 · INV-M02 · 15 sections · 14 min 03 Diversification and Common Risk Diversification reduces idiosyncratic risk but cannot remove the market and recession risks shared by all assets. Extension by module

These articles do not take a core-step slot; they fill in the station’s concepts, methods and historical background by module. Read the route first, then jump as needed.

E01 From Macro to Assets: Pricing Growth, Inflation and Policy Macro research is not forecasting one GDP number; it links growth, inflation, policy rates and risk premia into an asset-pricing chain. Real rates, earnings expectations and discount rates move separately, and asset prices rearrange; the same macro data mean different things for different assets. INV-028 · INV-M03 · 14 sections · 7 min E02 Industry and Company: Moats and Return on Capital Industry research asks where the money comes from and why it persists; company research asks whether a firm can turn industry profit into its own return on capital. A moat is not a brand slogan but a structure that stops competition from eroding returns: network, switching cost, scale, patents and location. INV-029 · INV-M04 · 15 sections · 8 min E03 Earnings Quality: Profit, Cash and Accruals Profit is an accounting measure; cash is an economic one. High accruals and low cash conversion can signal aggressive revenue recognition, working-capital bloat or one-off gains; earnings-quality analysis turns profit back into cash and capital and then tests durability. INV-030 · INV-M05 · 26 sections · 12 min E04 Investment Strategies An investment strategy is not a style label but a reproducible set of return sources, risk exposures, capacity limits and trading costs. INV-025 · INV-M09 · 12 sections · 10 min E05 Where Strategy Returns Come From: Beta, Factors and Capacity Any strategy return can be split into market beta, identifiable factors, genuine alpha and luck. Decomposing the sources shows whether it is replicable and whether capacity and fees will eat it; high return does not equal high skill. INV-031 · INV-M09 · 12 sections · 8 min E06 Transaction Cost Analysis: Impact, Slippage and VWAP/TWAP Trading cost is not just a commission; it is the gap between the decision price and execution price, made of delay, spread, impact and opportunity cost. INV-027 · INV-M10 · 23 sections · 8 min E07 Hedging and Stress Testing: The Cost of Protection Hedging does not remove risk; it changes its shape. Futures lock in a price but give up upside; options pay a premium but keep some upside. Stress testing asks whether protection still works in extreme scenarios. INV-032 · INV-M12 · 15 sections · 8 min E08 Performance Attribution: Where the Return Came From Performance attribution splits portfolio-minus-benchmark returns into allocation, selection and interaction: right sectors, right stocks, and their combination. Without attribution, performance is a story; with it, you can judge whether it is repeatable. INV-033 · INV-M13 · 13 sections · 8 min E09 Behavioral Biases and Investment Discipline: Knowing Is Not Doing The biggest risk for investors is sometimes not the market but their own behaviour: overtrading, the disposition effect, chasing returns, overconfidence and recency bias. Discipline is not a willpower slogan but an executable process of decision rules, position limits and checklists. INV-034 · INV-M14 · 14 sections · 7 min E10 The Household Balance Sheet: Human Capital, Housing and Pensions A household is not just “how much money it has”; it also has future wages, housing, pensions and liabilities. Human capital resembles a bond, housing a local asset, pensions a long-horizon fund; putting them on one sheet shows whether risk is concentrated. INV-035 · INV-M15 · 26 sections · 12 min E11 Responsible Investing and Professional Practice Responsible investing brings environmental, social and governance information into due diligence and portfolio constraints; professional practice puts suitability, conflicts and client assets where they can be checked. INV-026 · INV-M16 · 12 sections · 10 min E12 Responsible Investment and Professional Ethics: Writing Preferences into Portfolios Responsible investment is not “doing good earns more”; it turns environmental, social and governance preferences into executable screening, engagement and disclosure rules. Professional ethics put client interests, conflicts and suitability before sales. INV-036 · INV-M16 · 13 sections · 7 min E13 Building an Investment Research Process Turn an opinion into a traceable, falsifiable research file: state the question and falsification conditions, then check the 10-K, 8-K and EDGAR source documents; all example numbers are hypothetical. INV-005 · INV-M01 · 15 sections · 8 min E14 Real Returns and Purchasing Power Convert net nominal return into real return with the Fisher relation, and put fees, taxes and inflation in one calculation chain; all example numbers are hypothetical. INV-003 · INV-M02 · 13 sections · 8 min E15 Choosing an Investment Benchmark Choose the reference from goals and investable range first, then align return, fee and frequency conventions; all example numbers are hypothetical and a market index itself cannot be bought. INV-004 · INV-M02 · 15 sections · 8 min E16 Macro Scenario Analysis Turn a macro view into reproducible conditional paths: baseline, inflation-upside and recession cases mapped to portfolio exposures; all example numbers are hypothetical, not forecasts or advice. INV-006 · INV-M03 · 15 sections · 11 min E17 Industry Competition and Business Models Judge whether profits can persist through industry structure, unit economics and capital returns: start with the profit pool and entry barriers, then compute customer lifetime value and acquisition-cost payback; all example numbers are hypothetical. INV-007 · INV-M04 · 16 sections · 12 min E18 Earnings-Quality Analysis Judge whether reported profit is sustainable, cash-backed and comparable: reconcile net income to operating cash flow, then explain the gap through accruals, revenue quality, non-GAAP adjustments and notes; all example numbers are hypothetical. INV-008 · INV-M05 · 15 sections · 11 min E19 Cash-Flow Valuation: A Research Process Estimate a value range from future free cash flow, a discount rate and a terminal value: rebuild base cash flow, discount each year, then sensitize; all example numbers are hypothetical. INV-009 · INV-M06 · 15 sections · 12 min E20 Comparability in Relative Valuation The hard part of relative valuation is comparability, not the multiple: define the question and peer set, align dates, capital structure and accounting, then explain differences; all example numbers are hypothetical. INV-010 · INV-M06 · 14 sections · 8 min E21 Reverse Valuation and Expectations Treat the market price as given and solve for the growth or return it implies, then test whether those expectations are credible; all example numbers are hypothetical. INV-011 · INV-M06 · 14 sections · 7 min E22 Valuation Sensitivity and Scenarios Test how much a valuation conclusion depends on its key assumptions using sensitivity tables and probability-weighted scenarios; all example numbers are hypothetical. INV-012 · INV-M06 · 14 sections · 8 min E23 Trend, Price and Volume Use moving averages, closes and volume to test whether a trend exists, while stating lag, false breakouts and out-of-sample checks; the example is hypothetical data. INV-013 · INV-M07 · 15 sections · 11 min E24 Common Technical Indicators: Calculation and Limits Recompute SMA, EMA, MACD and the stochastic oscillator from the same hypothetical prices to show parameter effects, lag and conflicting signals. INV-014 · INV-M07 · 14 sections · 8 min E25 Economic Interpretation of Factor Strategies Use a hypothetical 12-month value factor series to show how factor portfolios are built, where returns may come from and how costs test the story, separating statistical from economic significance. INV-015 · INV-M08 · 15 sections · 11 min E26 Interpreting Backtest Results Use a hypothetical 60-month backtest to separate statistical significance, trading costs, multiple trials and out-of-sample evidence, so a historical fit is not mistaken for a future return. INV-016 · INV-M08 · 15 sections · 8 min E27 Trade Execution and Slippage Use a hypothetical 20,000-share buy to compare a CNY 50.00 decision price with a CNY 50.18 average fill, splitting 36 basis points of slippage, 3 basis points of commission and a 39 basis point implementation shortfall, and show the fill risk of a limit order. INV-022 · INV-M10 · 15 sections · 11 min E28 Asset Allocation and Risk Budgeting Use a two-asset hypothetical example to distinguish capital weights from risk contributions, compute volatility, marginal risk contribution and risk budgets, and show the limits imposed by leverage, correlation and estimation error. INV-018 · INV-M11 · 17 sections · 9 min E29 Position Sizing and Rebalancing Use a hypothetical CNY 1 million portfolio to size a position from a risk budget, trigger rebalancing at a 5 percentage point drift, and calculate the trade, cost and resulting exposure. INV-019 · INV-M11 · 16 sections · 9 min E30 Hedging: Purpose and Cost Use a hypothetical coffee purchase to show how futures and options lock a price, surrender part of the upside and leave basis risk, and how margin, financing, fees and basis change the effective price. INV-020 · INV-M12 · 15 sections · 10 min E31 Performance Evaluation and Attribution Compare a hypothetical 8.7% portfolio return with a 6.8% benchmark, deduct a 0.60% fee, and decompose the 1.30 percentage point net excess into allocation, selection and interaction effects while showing why one year cannot prove skill. INV-021 · INV-M13 · 16 sections · 12 min E32 Behavioral Biases and an Investment Journal Use a hypothetical journal of 30 decisions to record ex ante reasons, invalidation conditions and bias tags, then review adherence, holding-period gaps and outcome gaps instead of judging decision quality by profit alone. INV-023 · INV-M14 · 15 sections · 10 min E33 Household Wealth and Retirement Planning Use a hypothetical 40-year-old couple's balance sheet to convert a 70% replacement rate, 2.5% inflation and 25-year retirement into CNY 4.64 million of required assets, compare it with CNY 5.53 million projected, and show why a 119% funding ratio is not secure under return and longevity stress. INV-024 · INV-M15 · 15 sections · 11 min E34 The Investment Policy Statement: Writing Objectives, Constraints and Rebalancing into Rules An investment policy statement is not compliance decoration; it writes down why you invest, how much risk you can bear and when to adjust, in executable rules. Without one, portfolios drift with emotion, hype and short-term performance. INV-037 · INV-M01 · 12 sections · 9 min E35 The Macro Scenario Tree: Probabilities for Growth, Inflation and Policy Paths A macro forecast is not a point but a scenario tree: growth, inflation and policy each have paths, and probabilities determine expected value and tail risk. Writing scenarios as a tree shows where a forecast can go wrong. INV-038 · INV-M03 · 13 sections · 9 min E36 Industry Map: A Diligence Checklist from Value Chain to Capital Returns Industry research starts not with a story but with a map: who is upstream, who is downstream, where profit pools sit and why capital returns differ. Overlaying the value chain, entry barriers and capital intensity shows whether a company earns from industry structure or a temporary cycle. INV-039 · INV-M04 · 12 sections · 9 min E37 Factor Crowding: When the Same Alpha Becomes Collective Risk Factor crowding is not simply many investors using the same factor; it is many using the same trade to express it. When leverage, shorts and liquidity align, one redemption or deleveraging can reverse factor returns instantly. INV-040 · INV-M08 · 13 sections · 8 min E38 Hedge Ratio: Minimum Variance, Basis Risk and Tail Protection A hedge ratio is not '100% or nothing'; it is how much to hedge, with which instrument and how much basis risk to bear. The minimum-variance ratio cuts volatility but may sacrifice return; tail protection needs non-linear tools, not simply more shorts. INV-041 · INV-M12 · 14 sections · 8 min E39 Luck versus Skill: Evidence from Multi-Period Excess Returns One year of outperformance may be luck; a decade of consistent outperformance may be skill. Separating luck from skill needs sample length, t-statistics, persistence and attribution, not cumulative return alone. INV-042 · INV-M13 · 13 sections · 9 min E40 ESG Data Conflicts: Ratings, Disclosure and Portfolio Construction The same company can be rated A by one ESG provider and C by another because agencies disagree on what matters and how to score. Portfolio construction cannot rely on ratings alone; go back to raw disclosure, industry context and financial transmission. INV-043 · INV-M16 · 13 sections · 9 min
