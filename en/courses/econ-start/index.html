What this route solves

Three objectives, three kinds of judgement. The goal is not to recall terms but to explain a case, carry out a calculation and state a limit in order.

  • Understand scarcity and choice
  • Apply opportunity cost and marginal reasoning
  • Use supply and demand to explain price and quantity

Core route

Each step answers one sub-question. A concept introduced earlier is used again later; the order comes from the register, not from popularity.

01 What Economics Studies Economics studies how people choose under scarcity, and how those choices interact at the level of individuals, markets and the whole economy. ECO-001 · ECO-M01 · 13 sections · 12 min 02 Scarcity and Choice Scarcity means wants exceed what is available, so choice is unavoidable; the production possibilities frontier turns that trade-off into a boundary that can be calculated and compared. ECO-002 · ECO-M01 · 13 sections · 9 min 03 Opportunity Cost and Sunk Cost Opportunity cost measures the value of the next-best alternative; a sunk cost is already unrecoverable and should not keep driving the next decision. ECO-003 · ECO-M01 · 12 sections · 8 min 04 Margin and Incentives Marginal decisions compare the benefit and cost of one more unit, and incentives change those two quantities. ECO-004 · ECO-M01 · 13 sections · 7 min 05 Supply, Demand and Equilibrium Demand and supply curves relate price and quantity; equilibrium is their intersection, and movements along a curve differ from shifts of the curve. ECO-005 · ECO-M02 · 13 sections · 11 min

Extension by module

These articles do not take a core-step slot; they fill in the station’s concepts, methods and historical background by module. Read the route first, then jump as needed.

E01 Economies of Scale, Economies of Scope and Capacity Utilization Economies of scale come from spreading fixed costs over more output and from specialization; economies of scope come from sharing capabilities across products, and capacity utilization measures how far actual output is from sustainable maximum output. All three can lower cost, but all have limits: coordination cost, weak demand and quality loss can make “bigger” more expensive. ECO-035 · ECO-M03 · 12 sections · 9 min E02 Market Concentration, Entry Barriers and Platform Competition Market concentration describes how shares are distributed, entry barriers describe why new rivals struggle to enter, and platform competition adds network effects, multi-homing and switching costs to the same picture. High concentration does not automatically mean high prices, and low concentration does not automatically mean effective competition; market boundaries, entry conditions and actual conduct matter. ECO-036 · ECO-M04 · 12 sections · 9 min E03 Games and Information When outcomes depend on others' choices and one side knows more than the other, game theory and information economics provide testable equilibrium and contracting benchmarks. ECO-025 · ECO-M05 · 12 sections · 11 min E04 Information Asymmetry, Signaling and Mechanism Design When one side knows more, prices and contracts distort; signaling, screening and incentives try to reveal information through verifiable costs. ECO-034 · ECO-M05 · 17 sections · 10 min E05 Externalities, Pigouvian Taxes and Cap-and-Trade: When Price Tools Work Externalities let private cost omit social cost. A Pigouvian tax puts a price on the damage and feeds it back into decisions, while cap-and-trade fixes the quantity of emissions and lets the permit price adjust. Both can correct overproduction, but price certainty and quantity certainty cannot both be guaranteed; damage measurement, leakage and transaction cost decide when they work. ECO-038 · ECO-M06 · 11 sections · 8 min E06 Growth Accounting: Capital Deepening, Human Capital and TFP Growth accounting splits output growth into capital, labour, human capital and total factor productivity. It answers whether growth comes mainly from more inputs or from using inputs more efficiently, but it cannot treat the TFP residual as pure technological progress; measurement error, utilization, allocation and rents all sit inside it. ECO-037 · ECO-M09 · 18 sections · 9 min E07 Monetary Policy Transmission: Interest-Rate, Credit and Balance-Sheet Channels Monetary policy does not attach the policy rate directly to every borrower. It travels through funding cost, credit conditions, asset prices, the exchange rate and expectations to spending and inflation. Transmission is lagged, leaky and weakened by fixed-rate debt, bank capital and supply shocks. ECO-039 · ECO-M11 · 11 sections · 9 min E08 Fiscal Multipliers: Spending, Taxes and Automatic Stabilizers The fiscal multiplier is not a fixed constant. It is the feedback chain from a first-round shock to income, consumption and further income, reduced by leakages. Government purchases, tax cuts and transfers have different multipliers; automatic stabilizers cushion downturns, while openness, debt constraints and supply bottlenecks change the result. ECO-040 · ECO-M12 · 18 sections · 12 min E09 Tariffs, Quotas and Trade Agreements: The Welfare Ledger of Trade Barriers Tariffs and quotas both raise domestic prices, but tariff revenue goes to the government while quota rents go to licence holders. Trade agreements lower barriers among members while retaining rules costs. The welfare ledger must separate consumer losses, producer gains, fiscal revenue and efficiency losses. ECO-041 · ECO-M13 · 11 sections · 8 min E10 Labor, Population and Education Labor markets connect population, skills, migration and family decisions; wages and returns to education emerge from those forces together. ECO-026 · ECO-M15 · 12 sections · 10 min E11 Education Returns, Population Structure and Labour Participation Education raises productivity and participation, but returns depend on quality, matching, demography and measurement bias; a credential premium alone is not enough. ECO-033 · ECO-M15 · 17 sections · 10 min E12 Property Rights, State Capacity and the Institutional Conditions for Development Property rights lower the risk that an investment will be seized, while state capacity determines whether rules are enforced and whether taxes become public goods. Institutions are not abstract labels but a checklist: registration, contract enforcement, revenue collection, commitment and accountability. ECO-042 · ECO-M16 · 11 sections · 8 min E13 Industry, Innovation and the Digital Economy Innovation links R&D, diffusion, intellectual property and competition policy; the digital economy changes cost structures, network effects and how markets are entered. ECO-027 · ECO-M17 · 12 sections · 10 min E14 R&D, Diffusion and Platforms: Returns and Limits of Innovation Policy Social returns to R&D often exceed private returns, so knowledge spillovers leave private investment below the social optimum. Patents, subsidies, public research and standards are different tools. Platforms and AI add network effects, data and scale, changing how innovation gains are distributed; policy must weigh incentives, diffusion and entry together. ECO-043 · ECO-M17 · 11 sections · 9 min E15 Cities, Regions and Space Urban and regional economics puts distance, density and land in one framework: agglomeration brings efficiency, while congestion and land rents bring costs. ECO-028 · ECO-M18 · 12 sections · 10 min E16 Land, Commuting and Housing: The Price Ledger of Spatial Equilibrium Urban space is priced by three ledgers at once: access to jobs, the cost of commuting and congestion, and how housing supply responds to price. Put rent gradients, commuting costs and supply elasticity together, and the same urban growth can show up as higher prices in one city and longer commutes and lower density in another. ECO-044 · ECO-M18 · 11 sections · 9 min E17 Environment, Energy and Agriculture Environmental, energy and agricultural problems share a structure: resource use creates external costs, prices and property rights allocate them, and natural conditions limit how fast substitution can occur. ECO-029 · ECO-M19 · 12 sections · 10 min E18 Carbon Prices, Allowances and Abatement Costs: The Allocation Ledger of Climate Policy A carbon tax fixes the emissions price; an emissions trading system fixes the emissions total. Both push firms to abate where it is cheapest, but how allowances are allocated, how revenue is used and whether emissions leak decide who bears the cost behind the same carbon price. Climate policy is not a choice of tool name; it is an alignment of marginal abatement cost, coverage and complementary policy. ECO-045 · ECO-M19 · 11 sections · 9 min E19 Behavior, Experiments and Public Services Behavioral economics corrects the standard model with observable biases, while experiments and public services turn those biases into testable, retractable policy tools. ECO-030 · ECO-M20 · 12 sections · 10 min E20 Defaults, Nudges and Public Services: Effects and Limits of Behavioral Interventions Defaults and reminders can raise participation in public services, but effects found in a trial are not the effects that survive scale-up. Put average treatment effect, unit cost, sample size, persistence and spillovers on one ledger to judge whether an intervention is worth scaling or only works in a small setting. ECO-046 · ECO-M20 · 11 sections · 9 min E21 Law, Organizations and Comparative Economies Law and organizations decide who bears costs and who holds decision rights; comparative economics tests those rules across institutional traditions. ECO-031 · ECO-M21 · 12 sections · 10 min E22 Contracts, Specific Assets and Organizational Boundaries: A Transaction-Cost Ledger Markets, long-term contracts and internal organization are not three ideologies; they are three ways of handling transaction costs. The more specific the asset, the easier each party is locked in, and the more a sunk investment can be eroded by later renegotiation. Put contracting, monitoring, enforcement and bureaucratic costs on one ledger to decide whether a transaction belongs in the market, in a contract or inside a firm. ECO-047 · ECO-M21 · 11 sections · 9 min E23 China and the Global Economy China's relationship with the global economy is shaped by trade, investment, supply chains and institutional choices; no single indicator captures it. ECO-032 · ECO-M22 · 12 sections · 10 min E24 China's External Balance and Value-Chain Position: Reading the External Ledger A country's external ledger is more than a trade surplus. The current account equals saving minus investment, and net foreign assets are also changed by exchange rates and valuation; gross exports contain imported inputs, so only the value-added share shows what a country actually creates. Put saving-investment, value-added trade, net foreign assets and reserve cover on one ledger to read how China connects to the global economy. ECO-048 · ECO-M22 · 11 sections · 9 min E25 Elasticity of Demand Price elasticity of demand measures how responsive quantity demanded is to price, and so how revenue moves when a price changes. ECO-006 · ECO-M02 · 15 sections · 9 min E26 Utility and the Budget Constraint Utility measures the satisfaction a consumer gets from a bundle, while the budget constraint marks what is affordable; the best bundle equalizes marginal utility per yuan. ECO-007 · ECO-M02 · 13 sections · 9 min E27 Production and Cost A production function turns inputs into output, and costs depend on output and input prices. This article covers short-run cost and output decisions only. Marginal cost decides whether one more unit is worth making; average variable cost decides whether to shut down in the short run, and average total cost whether the price covers all costs. ECO-008 · ECO-M03 · 12 sections · 11 min E28 Competition and Monopoly A perfectly competitive firm takes the market price and produces where price equals marginal cost; a monopolist faces a downward-sloping demand curve, restricts output where marginal revenue equals marginal cost and charges a higher price. ECO-009 · ECO-M04 · 12 sections · 11 min E29 Externalities and Public Goods Externalities are effects on third parties that prices do not reflect, while public goods are nonexcludable and non-rival; both make market provision diverge from the efficient quantity. ECO-010 · ECO-M06 · 13 sections · 11 min E30 GDP and National Accounts GDP is the market value of final goods and services produced within a country in a period; production, income, and expenditure measures should give the same total. ECO-011 · ECO-M07 · 16 sections · 14 min E31 Nominal and Real Values Nominal values use current prices, while real values are adjusted with a price index; only real values support comparisons of quantities across periods. ECO-012 · ECO-M07 · 15 sections · 9 min E32 Consumption and Saving Consumption is household spending on final goods and services, and saving is disposable income not consumed; the saving rate measures that split, not a bank balance at a point in time. ECO-015 · ECO-M08 · 11 sections · 10 min E33 Investment and Capital Formation Capital formation measures acquisitions less disposals of produced assets on a gross basis, including fixed capital formation, inventories and valuables; only net capital formation equals an increase in the productive capital stock. National-accounts investment is fixed investment plus inventory change plus net acquisitions of valuables, and net investment subtracts depreciation, so positive gross investment does not guarantee a larger capital stock. ECO-016 · ECO-M08 · 11 sections · 9 min E34 The Business Cycle The business cycle alternates expansion and contraction; a recession runs from peak to trough, and the dates are usually assigned after the fact. ECO-018 · ECO-M08 · 14 sections · 8 min E35 Growth and Productivity Growth comes from more inputs or more output per input; sustained gains in output per person depend on labor productivity, not longer hours alone. ECO-017 · ECO-M09 · 12 sections · 11 min E36 Price Indices and Inflation A price index tracks the cost of a fixed basket, and inflation is its percentage change; the index level itself is not the inflation rate. ECO-013 · ECO-M10 · 12 sections · 11 min E37 Employment and Unemployment The unemployment rate divides the unemployed by the labor force, not by the whole population; it misses people who stop looking and people who want more hours. ECO-014 · ECO-M10 · 12 sections · 8 min E38 Monetary Policy Transmission Monetary policy affects aggregate demand through interest rates, credit, asset prices, exchange rates, and expectations, with lags and leakages from the policy rate to output and inflation. ECO-019 · ECO-M11 · 13 sections · 13 min E39 Fiscal Policy and the Multiplier Fiscal policy affects aggregate demand through spending and taxes; the multiplier magnifies an initial change in spending, but leakages, crowding out, and supply limits constrain it. ECO-020 · ECO-M12 · 13 sections · 12 min E40 Comparative Advantage and Trade Comparative advantage ranks producers by opportunity cost, not absolute productivity; even when one side is more efficient in every good, specialization and trade can benefit both. ECO-023 · ECO-M13 · 13 sections · 14 min E41 The Balance of Payments The balance of payments records all economic transactions between a country's residents and nonresidents in a period; a current account deficit is matched by net foreign capital inflow. ECO-021 · ECO-M14 · 13 sections · 11 min E42 Exchange-Rate Regimes and the Open Economy An exchange-rate regime determines whether the nominal rate is set by the market, a rule, or official intervention; the core tradeoff is between exchange-rate stability and monetary policy independence. ECO-022 · ECO-M14 · 13 sections · 9 min E43 Income Distribution and Inequality Income distribution describes how income is spread across households; quintile shares, the Lorenz curve, and the Gini coefficient measure inequality, but definitions shape the conclusion. ECO-024 · ECO-M16 · 13 sections · 13 min E44 Public Goods and Common-Pool Resources: Why Voluntary Provision Fails Public goods are non-excludable and non-rival; common-pool resources are rival but hard to exclude. Both make free riding and overuse the default. This article puts the Samuelson condition, the voluntary-provision gap and common-pool quotas into one account, and shows which part government, clubs and community rules can each solve. ECO-049 · ECO-M06 · 12 sections · 10 min E45 Three Phillips Curves: Wages, Expectations and Supply Shocks The Phillips curve is not one line with a fixed slope. The wage-bargaining version trades inflation against unemployment; the expectations-augmented version removes anticipated inflation from the trade-off; the supply-shock version shifts the whole curve. Only the three together explain why the same unemployment rate can come with high or low inflation. ECO-050 · ECO-M10 · 12 sections · 9 min E46 Balance-Sheet Runoff and the Rate Corridor: How Central-Bank Assets Transmit Quantitative tightening is not simply withdrawing liquidity. Bond runoff on the asset side, reserves and overnight reverse repos on the liability side, and the corridor's floor and ceiling jointly determine whether short rates stay controlled. This article separates the T-account, reserve supply and demand, and the three corridor segments, then calculates a runoff shock. ECO-051 · ECO-M11 · 12 sections · 9 min E47 Who Pays the Tariff: Intermediate Goods, Rules of Origin and Value Chains The legal tariff payer is the importer, but the economic burden is shared by consumers, importers, exporters and upstream suppliers. Intermediate goods, rules of origin and value chains turn a simple tariff line into an effective rate of protection, compliance cost and double taxation. This article uses the effective-protection formula and a three-country value-chain example to separate who really pays. ECO-052 · ECO-M13 · 15 sections · 8 min E48 Balance-of-Payments Crises: Reserves, Capital Flows and Exchange Regimes A balance-of-payments crisis is not the trade deficit itself, but a maturity mismatch among usable reserves, short-term external debt and capital flows. A fixed rate turns exchange-rate risk into reserve risk; a float turns reserve risk into price risk. This article uses reserve-cover ratios and a sudden-stop example to separate the trigger sequence. ECO-053 · ECO-M14 · 14 sections · 8 min E49 Platform Entry Barriers: Data, Network Effects and Interoperability Platform entry barriers are not just 'many users'. Network effects, data feedback, switching costs and multi-homing jointly determine whether a new platform can reach minimum viable scale. This article puts two-sided subsidies, the data flywheel and interoperability duties into one entry account, and explains why antitrust looks at contestability rather than market share alone. ECO-054 · ECO-M17 · 14 sections · 8 min E50 Housing Affordability: Land, Commuting and Zoning Housing affordability is not one price-to-income ratio; it is the combined result of land supply, zoning density, commuting cost and local public finance. This article links developable land, floor-area ratio, commuting radius and the affordable range, explaining why the same income buys very different housing in different parts of a city. ECO-055 · ECO-M18 · 15 sections · 8 min E51 System Costs of the Energy Transition: Grids, Storage and Capacity Markets Falling levelised costs of wind and solar do not make the whole power system cheaper. Intermittent supply needs grid expansion, storage, balancing and capacity backup, and these system costs are not in the module price. This article puts LCOE, system cost and capacity markets into one account and calculates the true cost of a 60% renewable system. ECO-056 · ECO-M19 · 14 sections · 9 min E52 Scaling Behavioural Interventions: When Defaults Cross the Line Defaults, salience and social norms can work in randomised trials, but that does not mean they survive scale-up. This article puts effect size, reach, implementation cost and ethical boundaries into one account, explaining why opt-out defaults raise participation while potentially denying choice without users' knowledge. ECO-057 · ECO-M20 · 14 sections · 9 min E53 Law Enforcement and Market Trust: The Testable Part of Comparative Institutions Law promotes market trust not through the quality of statutes but through clear property rights, enforceable contracts and calculable breach costs. This article uses transaction costs, enforcement costs and trust substitution to open the 'good institutions, fast growth' black box and keep only the testable part. ECO-058 · ECO-M21 · 13 sections · 9 min E54 Global Spillovers from China's Manufacturing Upgrade: Intermediates, Demand and Rules China's manufacturing upgrade is not simply 'exporting more final goods'; it changes global production networks through intermediates, capital goods, demand spillovers and rule participation. This article separates forward linkages, backward linkages and demand spillovers into three calculable channels, showing who gains, who adjusts and who faces competitive pressure.
