What this route solves

Three objectives, three kinds of judgement. The goal is not to recall terms but to explain a case, carry out a calculation and state a limit in order.

  • Distinguish primary texts from later interpretation
  • Recognize hindsight bias
  • Compare bubbles and crises

Core route

Each step answers one sub-question. A concept introduced earlier is used again later; the order comes from the register, not from popularity.

01 How to Read Economic History and the History of Economic Thought Reading economic history requires separating primary texts, editions, context and later interpretation; reconstruct what was knowable before judging outcomes or drawing analogies. HIS-001 · HIS-M01 · 15 sections · 12 min 02 Historical Analogies and Hindsight Bias Compare mechanisms, institutions and scale before asking whether “this time looks like then”; outcome knowledge makes the past seem more predictable than it was. HIS-002 · HIS-M01 · 16 sections · 8 min 03 Reading Bubbles and Crises Comparatively Comparing 1929 and 2008 starts with what was knowable at the time, then examines mechanisms, institutions, policy and outcomes rather than drawdown alone. HIS-024 · HIS-M16 · 14 sections · 16 min 04 Classical Political Economy From Smith and Malthus to Ricardo, classical political economy asked how wealth grows, what determines value, and how output is divided; this article reads the primary texts in context and marks the limits of interpretation. Extension by module

These articles do not take a core-step slot; they fill in the station’s concepts, methods and historical background by module. Read the route first, then jump as needed.

E01 From Aristotle to Aquinas: Wealth, Interest and the Boundaries of Exchange Early economic thought was not “ancient economics”; it discussed whether exchange is just, money is natural and interest is legitimate within the frames of household, city and salvation. Aristotle separated livelihood from unlimited acquisition; Aquinas placed usury, the just price and fraud inside medieval theology and law. HIS-028 · HIS-M02 · 14 sections · 8 min E02 Marx’s Circuit of Capital: From Capital to Socialist Accounting Marx treated capital as a movement through changing forms: money buys means of production and labour, production creates commodities, and commodities sell for more money. Surplus value, the organic composition of capital and reproduction schemes were his core tools for explaining profit and crisis. HIS-029 · HIS-M04 · 12 sections · 7 min E03 The Marginal Revolution: Jevons, Menger and Walras on Exchange The marginal revolution shifted economics from “how much is produced” to “how much is one more unit worth”. Jevons, Menger and Walras almost simultaneously found that value comes from marginal utility and equilibrium from exchange ratios; Marshall then turned marginal analysis into a teachable price theory. HIS-030 · HIS-M05 · 15 sections · 8 min E04 After Keynes: Monetarism, New Classical and New Keynesian Economics Keynes brought effective demand, uncertainty and policy space into macroeconomics; postwar debates turned on how potent fiscal policy is, how much money matters, and how expectations change rules. Monetarism, new classical and new Keynesian economics are not three slogans but three models of shocks, expectations and policy response. HIS-031 · HIS-M06 · 14 sections · 8 min E05 Commons Governance and Institutional Analysis Common-pool resources are not solved only by nationalisation or privatisation; boundaries, rules, monitoring and graduated sanctions can support polycentric governance. HIS-026 · HIS-M07 · 23 sections · 8 min E06 Regional and Comparative History Regional and comparative history places experiences from different continents under the same questions; the point is not who came first but how conditions, institutions and measurement differ. HIS-025 · HIS-M10 · 14 sections · 8 min E07 The Great Divergence and Institutional Comparison: Evidence from Regional History Why did Western Europe industrialise first in the nineteenth century while other regions did not? The Great Divergence debate puts geography, institutions, colonisation, energy and knowledge networks on one comparative table. This article adds representative experiences from Europe and the Americas, Asia, Africa and the Middle East, then sets out four checks for institutional comparison. HIS-032 · HIS-M10 · 12 sections · 11 min E08 From Coin to Central Bank: A History of Banking, Insurance and Payments Modern financial intermediaries were not designed in one go. Coin, bills, deposits, insurance and pensions solved different problems in different eras: payment, long-distance settlement, risk pooling and old-age support. Central banks acquired note issue, lender-of-last-resort and supervisory functions through crises and wars. HIS-033 · HIS-M12 · 15 sections · 8 min E09 War Finance and Sovereign Debt: The Ledger of State Capacity Sovereign debt is not simply “government borrowing”; it is an intertemporal exchange among war, taxation and credit. The debt ratio depends on interest rates, growth and the primary balance; whether a state can borrow, for how long and in what currency reflects state capacity and credible commitment. HIS-034 · HIS-M13 · 15 sections · 8 min E10 From Mutual Funds to Indexing and Quant: The Institutionalisation of Investment Thought Investment thought expanded from “picking good companies” to portfolios, indices, factors and quant models. Mutual funds made diversification a mass product, index funds made low-cost beta replicable, and factor research decomposed return sources into testable exposures. HIS-035 · HIS-M15 · 23 sections · 9 min E11 Comparing Bubbles: 1637, 1720, 1929 and 2008 Four famous bubbles differ in assets, leverage and institutions; compare mechanisms, not overlaid price curves. HIS-027 · HIS-M16 · 21 sections · 9 min E12 Early Economic Thought From Aristotle to Aquinas, early economic thought asked how households acquire goods, when exchange is fair, and why interest is suspect; this article reads the primary texts in context and marks the limits of interpretation. HIS-003 · HIS-M02 · 15 sections · 10 min E13 Mercantilism and Physiocracy Mercantilism and physiocracy both asked where wealth comes from and what the state should do, but answered differently; this article compares their contexts, texts, criticisms and later misreadings. HIS-004 · HIS-M03 · 15 sections · 12 min E14 The Formation of Marxist Economics From the 1848 Manifesto to Capital Volume I in 1867, Marx analysed capitalism as a historical system; this article traces the texts, core arguments, debates and later misreadings. HIS-006 · HIS-M04 · 14 sections · 10 min E15 The Marginal Revolution and Neoclassical Economics From the 1870s, Jevons, Menger and Walras shifted value theory toward marginal utility, and Marshall combined utility, cost and time; this article sets out the claims, evidence and limits of that change. HIS-007 · HIS-M05 · 14 sections · 12 min E16 The Keynesian Revolution and Postwar Debates From the monetary debates of the 1920s through the General Theory, the Employment Act of 1946, the Phillips curve and stagflation, this article examines the texts, policies and disputes of the Keynesian revolution, distinguishing Keynes himself from later "Keynesianism". HIS-008 · HIS-M06 · 14 sections · 10 min E17 Institutional, Behavioral and Pluralist Traditions From Veblen's critique of institutions to Simon's bounded rationality, Kahneman and Tversky's prospect theory and Ostrom's polycentric governance, this article shows how these traditions challenge the rational-actor model, and sets out the method and the limits of each. HIS-009 · HIS-M07 · 14 sections · 10 min E18 Agriculture, Commerce and Early Markets From Domesday (1086) to eighteenth-century agricultural commercialisation, this article examines how farming, trade and market institutions evolved in Europe, mainly England, and where continuity and rupture lie. HIS-010 · HIS-M08 · 14 sections · 10 min E19 The Industrial Revolution and Long-Run Growth From eighteenth-century British technology and factories to cross-country catch-up by 1913, this article asks why the Industrial Revolution happened, how growth accelerated, and where the "revolution" narrative strains against long-run statistics. HIS-011 · HIS-M08 · 16 sections · 8 min E20 Globalization, Trade and Divergence From Ricardo to twenty-first-century trade openness, this article examines how globalization widened and then partly narrowed income gaps between countries, separating theoretical gains from historical distribution. HIS-012 · HIS-M08 · 15 sections · 8 min E21 Economic Institutions and Commerce in Imperial China From Northern Song paper money to Ming-Qing silver taxation, this article examines land, money and market institutions in imperial China and the tension between state capacity and commercial expansion. HIS-013 · HIS-M09 · 15 sections · 10 min E22 China's Modern Economic Transition From the five treaty ports of 1842 to the renminbi of 1948, this article examines how treaty tariffs, modern industry and currency unification reshaped China's economy, and where the statistics and interpretations end. HIS-014 · HIS-M09 · 15 sections · 8 min E23 China's Modern Industrialization From 12 billion yuan of industrial value added in 1952 to 31.7% of GDP in 2023, this article examines the stages of Chinese industrialization, the statistical definitions, and the efficiency questions behind output. HIS-015 · HIS-M09 · 17 sections · 9 min E24 Reform, Opening and Market Development From 1978 to 2023, this article examines how reform and opening widened markets and changed trade and growth, and how policy, data and interpretation should be kept apart. HIS-016 · HIS-M09 · 17 sections · 9 min E25 The Evolution of Monetary Systems From commodity money and coinage to convertible paper and fiat money, how issuers, redemption promises and credibility changed. HIS-017 · HIS-M11 · 14 sections · 9 min E26 Gold Standard, Bretton Woods and Floating Rates From the classical gold standard to Bretton Woods and the closing of the gold window in 1971, how fixed rates, convertibility and adjustment changed. HIS-020 · HIS-M11 · 15 sections · 11 min E27 A History of Banking and Central Banks From Stockholms Banco to the Bank Charter Act and the creation of the Federal Reserve, this article covers the verifiable milestones, changing functions and early constraints of banking and central-bank institutions. HIS-018 · HIS-M12 · 16 sections · 11 min E28 Public Debt and Fiscal History How war, taxation and growth shaped public debt levels and debt management in Britain and the United States, from the late seventeenth century to 2025. HIS-019 · HIS-M13 · 15 sections · 12 min E29 A History of Joint-Stock Companies and Securities Markets From the Bank of England's charter in 1694 and the South Sea Bubble to the 1792 Buttonwood Agreement and the 1929 crash, how companies, exchanges and regulation shaped one another. HIS-021 · HIS-M14 · 15 sections · 10 min E30 A History of Futures, Options and Risk Transfer From the Chicago Board of Trade in 1848 to the CFTC in 1974 and options on futures in 1982, showing how standard contracts, margin and clearing made price risk tradable. HIS-023 · HIS-M14 · 15 sections · 8 min E31 A History of Funds, Indices and Investment Thought From the first U.S. open-end mutual fund in 1924 to the first index fund for individual investors in 1976, how indices, funds and investment ideas reinforced one another; the disputed start year of the price averages could not be checked against a source and is kept only as this article's note. HIS-022 · HIS-M15 · 15 sections · 10 min E32 Silk, Silver and Tribute: Money and Governance in Eurasian Trade Networks The Silk Road was not a single road but a network of oases, steppe routes, seaports and tribute missions. Silk, silver and tribute gifts served monetary, diplomatic and governance functions at different nodes; understanding the network explains the rise and fall of Eurasian trade. HIS-036 · HIS-M02 · 14 sections · 9 min E33 From the Corn Laws to Free Trade: The Ledger of British Policy Debate The Corn Laws were not simply landlords versus manufacturers; they were a distributional ledger of land rents, wages, industrial profits and food security. Repeal lowered food prices and reshaped Britain's political coalitions and global trade. HIS-037 · HIS-M03 · 14 sections · 9 min E34 The Socialist Calculation Debate: Prices, Planning and Information The calculation debate of the 1920s-30s was not an ideological slogan but an information problem: without market prices, can planners know where resources should go? Mises and Hayek stressed dispersed information; Lange proposed simulated prices and trial-and-error rules. HIS-038 · HIS-M04 · 14 sections · 9 min E35 From Marginal Utility to Welfare Economics: Pareto, Pigou and Arrow Welfare economics starts from utility and asks what counts as a good allocation. Pareto efficiency, Pigouvian externalities and Arrow's impossibility theorem define the boundaries of efficiency, equity and collective choice. HIS-039 · HIS-M05 · 15 sections · 9 min E36 The Resource Curse in Africa and the Middle East: Institutions, Rents and Conflict Resource abundance does not guarantee growth: rents concentrate, institutions get captured and conflict gets financed. Africa and the Middle East show that the resource curse is not geological destiny but a result of institutions, rent distribution and external shocks. HIS-040 · HIS-M10 · 16 sections · 9 min E37 Historical Echoes of Digital Money: From Notes and Bills to CBDC Digital money is not entirely new: notes, bills, bank deposits and credit cards all did the same thing, turning money from an object into a transferable accounting claim. CBDC differs in the issuer being the central bank, the liability being a central-bank liability, and programmability. HIS-041 · HIS-M11 · 17 sections · 9 min E38 The Origins of Insurance: Marine Insurance, Mutual Aid and Actuarial Science Insurance did not begin with risk aversion but with maritime merchants sharing losses on ships and cargo. Marine insurance, mutual societies and life annuities turned random losses into priceable premiums; actuarial science made mortality and the law of large numbers commercial foundations. HIS-042 · HIS-M12 · 18 sections · 8 min E39 Sovereign Debt Restructuring: From the 1930s to the 2020s Sovereign debt restructuring is not 'defaulting' but a process where creditors and debtors reallocate losses. The 1930s Depression, 1980s Latin American crisis, 1990s HIPC and 2010s Greece show different mechanisms and costs. HIS-043 · HIS-M13 · 17 sections · 9 min
